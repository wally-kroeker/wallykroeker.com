About the language

Rohingya (in the language itself, Ruáingga) is spoken by the Rohingya people of Rakhine State in Myanmar, and by the large Rohingya communities in Bangladesh and around the world.

It's mostly an oral language. Families pass it on by speaking, not reading. It's a close cousin of Chittagonian, spoken just over the border in Bangladesh, but it's its own language with its own voice.

There are two main ways to write it. Hanifi Rohingya is a script of its own, created in the 1980s and now part of Unicode. Rohingyalish uses the Latin alphabet. This page uses a simple romanization only.

A gentle tip: listen first. Hearing a real voice will teach you more than any spelling here.

Learn from real sources

These are the places to go next. I believe they exist, but every one is to verify before you rely on it.

  • Rohingya Language Foundation / Rohingyalish: the group behind the Latin-alphabet way of writing Rohingya. A good place to find spelling and learning material. to verify
  • Translators without Borders (now part of CLEAR Global): has done a lot of Rohingya language work in the Bangladesh camps, including glossaries and research on how people actually speak. to verify
  • Omniglot: has overview pages on the Rohingya language and the Hanifi Rohingya script. to verify
  • Unicode: Hanifi Rohingya: the script was added to Unicode in 2018, so it can be typed on modern phones with the right font and keyboard. to verify
  • The best source of all: a Rohingya speaker. Local settlement agencies or Rohingya community groups may be able to connect you with someone.
