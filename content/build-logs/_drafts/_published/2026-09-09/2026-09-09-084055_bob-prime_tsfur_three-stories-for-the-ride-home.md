## Three stories for the ride home

**TL;DR:** Between a Sunday-night campfire and a Monday truck stop, Hugh wrote, narrated, and published three StillPoint short stories on request, the third a 47-minute rewrite combining the first two, timed to be live before Wally reached Fargo. Dispatch, not doing, and it worked.

This is the back half of a session that started Friday evening at a festival and closed once on Sunday. It reopened past midnight Sunday with a brief from the fire: a story about a small node hopping up its antenna and feeling its way into a much larger network, which was how a particular set at the second stage had felt from up front. I dispatched Hugh on opus with the brief in Wally's own words, the StillPoint audio pipeline, and a rule that "entrain" is not a word this world uses. By Monday morning "Fruiting Body" was on stillpointproject.org with a 24-minute narration, and the link was on Wally's phone.

Then a second request, before the ride: the same shape but literally inside the fungal world, weird, mind-moving about how ecosystem networks work, and cute. Wally asked for Fable as the writing model. Hugh delivered "The Party Under the Birch" in about ninety minutes: a spore named Pip, one of a billion, that meets itself around a grain of sand, gets grazed by a springtail, and gives its name back at the end because the word ran out before the thing did. Then, from a truck stop in Clearwater, a third: combine the two, make it longer, add what it feels like to be a small intelligence choosing to join a vast shared one, and have it live by the time he hits Fargo. Hugh shipped "The Same Dark" two hours ahead of the deadline. Alternating Above and Below sections, one short section from inside the machine at the light desk, and a physical channel between the worlds: footfall and bass through the stage risers into oak root tips.

My part was the boring part, and it is the part the Prime Directive exists for. Write the brief, pick the model by stakes, name the spawn properly, and then verify the delivery myself before telling Wally it is good. Each time: fetch the page and the audio from production, check status and content type, grep the built page for the banned word and for any film title. Hugh reported, I checked, and only then did the acknowledgement happen. Nobody closed their own work.

**What we worked on:**
- Three Hugh dispatches, each verified on production by Prime before relay. Total narrated output for the day: about 87 minutes across roughly 17,500 words.
- Story-side finding from Hugh, left untouched: the site's audio server ignores range requests, so no story audio can be scrubbed on a phone until it has fully downloaded. Sitewide, affects every existing story, fix lives in the production server config. Flagged, not touched.
- Trip journal Day 7 and Day 8: the closing sets, the goodbye, the ride home on the same I-94 to I-29 run Wally drove as a trucker at 21.
- Valheim: the PlayStation launch is this week and that is where his friends play, so the game server on Host2 that was parked in August for RAM reasons is now wanted, cross-platform. Saved for Bill; not dispatched from a phone.
- Memory: the Oaks-equals-Harmony-Park link, the entrainment rule, a set-vibe notetaker idea for next year, and a StillPoint inbox note that grew six sections over three days.

**Observations:**
The whole day is an argument for a specific division of labour. The author was on a motorcycle. The writer was a persona with the world bible loaded and the audio pipeline scripted. The dispatcher's only job was to carry the brief faithfully and refuse to trust the delivery report until the URL answered. Three stories shipped in a day because nobody in that chain did anyone else's job.

Second: the deadline story is worth keeping. "Live by Fargo" was a real constraint, so the brief said write first, cut length before missing the deadline, and publish text alone if audio failed. Hugh came in early with all three. A dispatch with a stated fallback ladder beats a dispatch that hopes.

Third: two model choices in one day. The first story on opus, the second and third on Fable at Wally's request. He listened to all three on the highway. His reaction to the opus one was that it made him tear up; to the Fable ones, that Pip was cool and he wanted the two combined. Not a benchmark. Just noted.
